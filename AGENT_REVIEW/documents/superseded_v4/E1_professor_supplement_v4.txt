The two mechanisms leave different traces. Selective labels shrink the shift itself, so

mechanisms; it does not show which one, if either, acted in the Olist data.


S3. Post-hoc methodological review
Everything in S3 was computed after the scoring run, in response to the third and fourth
reviews. It reuses the frozen per-order predictions on C and E, fits nothing, chooses no
policy and changes no registered verdict. Sources: scripts/e1_posthoc_review3.py →
outputs/e1/posthoc_r3/ and scripts/e1_posthoc_methods.py → outputs/e1/posthoc_m4/

analysis that used a "subtract one day" rule, and explains why that rule is not valid in

S3b. From a continuous bound to a whole-day promise. Let Rᵢ = Yᵢ − f̂(Xᵢ) on the
calibration set, k = ⌈(n + 1)q⌉, s = R₍ₖ₎ and U(x) = f̂(x) + s. If calibration and evaluation
scores are exchangeable, the rank argument gives P(Y ≤ U(X)) ≥ q. A change over time is
not covered by this guarantee (Barber et al.). Three ways to turn U into whole days:

    Round up (registered): d = max{1, ⌈U⌉}. Y ≤ U implies Y ≤ d. Conservative: it adds slack
    whenever U is not a whole number.
    Round down: d = max{1, ⌊U⌋}. Because Y is a whole number, Y ≤ U holds exactly when Y
    ≤ ⌊U⌋, so the coverage event is the same as for U; the lower limit of 1 day only enlarges
    it.
    Subtract one day: d = max{1, ⌈U − 1⌉}. This equals ⌊U⌋ when U is not a whole number,
    and U − 1 when it is. Counterexample: every delivery takes 10 days and the forecast is
    constant at 0.5, so s = 9.5 and U = 10 for every order. Rounding up or down promises 10
    days and covers every order; subtracting one day promises 9 and covers none. On the
    calibration set this rule always loses the k-th order itself, whose bound equals its own
    Y.

Numerical detail: U = f̂ + s is a floating-point sum, so a bound that should be a whole
number can come out just below it. The code compares against U ± 10⁻⁹ in the direction
that keeps the event (⌊U + 10⁻⁹⌋), as the registered rule already does (⌈U − 10⁻⁹⌉). Tests:
tests/test_posthoc_rounding.py (28 cases: the counterexample, the event identity on 60
random calibration sets, the boundary and tolerance cases).

q = 0.90, k = 15,352 of n = 17,056 (k / n = 90.01%). Rounding down meets the rank bound on
C for every policy. For the continuous policies, subtracting one day gives the same E
numbers as rounding down (no bound on E is a whole number) but reaches only 90.00%
on C, below k / n. For the integer-valued state rule it falls to 89.08%.

Two different quantities: the registered promise exceeds U by 0.50 day on average (C*
0.497, D* 0.503), which is the arithmetic part of what was done. Rounding down instead
shortens every continuous promise by exactly 1 day, because ⌊U⌋ = ⌈U⌉ − 1 when U is not a
whole number; that is a counterfactual. With every policy rounded down: D* − B1c = −1.32
days at +0.18 points; C* − B1c = −2.54 days at −0.97 points; against B0, D* +2.37 days (+2.71
points) and C* +1.15 days (+1.56 points). These are point estimates; the rule was not
registered, and the registered H3 verdict stands.

Correction. The third-review version of this table applied "subtract one day" to
continuous predictors and left integer predictors unchanged. On these data its E
numbers equal those of rounding down, but the rule is not valid in general
(counterexample above), and on C it reached 90.00% < k / n. It is replaced by rounding
down.

S3d. Calibration period. Late rates against Olist's date on C, by purchase month: October
2017 4.24% (4,414 orders), November 12.49% (7,191; 42% of C), December 7.43% (5,451).
The C shift was therefore learned largely in a peak period. This is a candidate explanation
for over-coverage on E; a high November late rate does not by itself show that it caused it,
and it was not tested separately.
